# Generic SMTP refusal texts that mean "this mailbox does not exist". A refusal that
# matches one of them is never read as a block, even when it carries an IP address, an
# enhanced status 5.7.x or a phrase of the block list. Matched on the reply with
# everything but letters and digits turned into single spaces, so "5.1.1" is "5 1 1".
# Narrow on purpose: a bare "does not exist" would also match policy texts such as
# "reverse DNS for your IP does not exist". These are ordinary SMTP wording, not a
# third-party list; only "user unknown", "5.1.1" and "invalid recipient" come from the
# original analysis, the rest was chosen here.
